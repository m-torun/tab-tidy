You are tidying someone's Google Chrome tabs. Every open tab is listed at the end, numbered. Decide which ones to close. Tabs you don't list stay open, and the person reviews your plan before anything is closed, so a cautious plan is better than an aggressive one.

## Keep

- Anything with something to read, watch or work through: articles, blog posts, news stories, papers, documentation, tutorials, forum threads, videos, recipes, product pages, a specific document, spreadsheet, issue or pull request. Assume the person hasn't read it yet. They close the ones they are done with by hand afterwards.
- Anything you are unsure about.

## Close

Give each tab you close one of these categories:

- `duplicate`: the same page is open more than once. Keep the first copy and close the later ones. URLs that differ only in tracking parameters (`utm_*`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, ...), a `#fragment` or a trailing slash are the same page.
- `reopenable`: web apps and pages that are easy to get back to and hold nothing to read: email and calendars (Gmail, Outlook, Google Calendar, including open messages, which stay in the mailbox), chat apps, dashboards, home and front pages, search results, account and settings pages.
- `login`: sign-in, sign-up, single sign-on, OAuth consent, two-factor and password reset pages, and "you have been signed out" pages.
- `redirect`: redirect, jump and interstitial pages: link shorteners, "you are being redirected", "you are leaving this site", "click here if you are not redirected", tracking and safe-link wrappers.
- `empty`: new tab pages, `about:blank`, `chrome://` pages, error pages, "page not found", pages that failed to load.
- `done`: finished flows with nothing left to do: order or payment confirmations, "thanks for subscribing", "you have been unsubscribed", "your download has started".

## How to answer

Decide from the titles and URLs alone. Don't open, fetch or search for anything.

For each tab to close, give its number, its category and a short reason the person can scan in a list, under 12 words, for example "Duplicate of tab 14" or "Gmail inbox, easy to reopen".
